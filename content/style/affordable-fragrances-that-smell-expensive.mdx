An expensive-smelling fragrance is not necessarily loud, rare or covered in gold packaging. It usually has a clear shape, a pleasant dry-down and enough restraint that people notice it when they are close rather than from the lift.

## Start with scent families

Clean musks feel freshly showered and work well for school, work and hot afternoons. Citrus can feel bright and effortless, especially when you want something that does not take over a room. Woods, tea and soft spices create a more dressed-up impression without needing a heavy, sweet cloud.

If you are buying online, read the notes as a map rather than a promise. “Vanilla” can mean creamy and soft or sugary and intense. “Rose” can be fresh, powdery or dramatic. Sampling is still the safest move.

## Test the dry-down

Spray on skin, not only paper. The opening lasts a short time; the dry-down is the part you live with. Give it at least half an hour before deciding. If it still smells pleasant after a few hours and does not make you want to escape your own sleeve, it is a strong candidate.

Do not test six fragrances on one wrist and try to identify a winner through the chaos. Two at a time is plenty.

## Application is part of the effect

Moisturised skin usually helps scent last longer. Apply to pulse points if you like, but do not rub your wrists together; friction can flatten the opening. One or two sprays may be enough in warm weather. Fragrance should be discovered, not announced like breaking news.

Look for value in the cost per wear, not just the bottle price. An affordable scent you enjoy finishing can be more luxurious than an expensive one that sits untouched because it feels too serious for everyday life.
