If your skin gets shiny by lunch and breaks out when you try something new, the answer is usually not a twelve-step routine. Oily, acne-prone skin still needs a calm barrier, and a calm barrier likes consistency more than drama.

## Start with four basics

Morning and night, use a gentle cleanser that removes sweat and sunscreen without leaving your face tight. Follow with a lightweight, fragrance-free moisturiser. In the morning, finish with a broad-spectrum sunscreen. At night, stop after moisturiser unless you are introducing one treatment.

That is enough to begin. Add complexity only when you know how your skin reacts.

## Choose one active

Salicylic acid can help with clogged pores because it works inside oily buildup. Benzoyl peroxide can help with acne-causing bacteria, but it may be drying and can bleach fabric. Adapalene is another evidence-backed option for persistent acne, but it is worth speaking with a pharmacist or clinician about how to use it safely.

Pick one. Use it slowly. Do not stack every acne ingredient in the same week because a burning face is not proof that a product is working.

## What to skip at first

Skip harsh scrubs, aggressive brushes, strong fragrance and constant product switching. A product can be popular and still be wrong for your skin. Patch-test where practical, introduce one change at a time, and give a routine several weeks before judging it.

If acne is painful, scarring, sudden or affecting your confidence, professional advice is the better move. Skincare content can help you build habits; it cannot diagnose you.

## The beginner routine

AM: gentle cleanser, lightweight moisturiser, sunscreen.

PM: gentle cleanser, one chosen treatment on its recommended schedule, moisturiser.

The luxury version of skincare is not owning more bottles. It is knowing what each product does, using it consistently and giving your skin a chance to settle.
