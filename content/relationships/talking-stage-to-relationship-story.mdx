Nora had been "just talking" to Adam for ninety-eight days.

She knew this because her Notes app contained a list titled:

**Things I Am Definitely Not Overthinking**

The list had twenty-six entries.

This was already evidence against the title.

Number four:

**He texts good morning literally every day.**

Number eleven:

**He knows my coffee order.**

Number seventeen:

**His mother knows my name????**

And number twenty-six, added at 1:14 a.m. on a Thursday:

**At what point does "talking" become a tax category?**

Nora was twenty-four.

Adam was twenty-five.

They behaved like a couple.

They simply had not completed the small administrative task of admitting it.

Modern romance is full of these efficiencies.

We have instant messaging, location sharing, video calls and the ability to send somebody a photograph of our dinner from another continent.

Yet asking:

**What are we?**

remains apparently beyond current technology.

## It started with a charger

![A couple talking together over coffee](https://images.pexels.com/photos/1024967/pexels-photo-1024967.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600 "A casual conversation that starts to feel like something more")

Nora met Adam at her coworker's birthday dinner.

She had forgotten her power bank.

Adam had a charger.

This was the complete romantic origin story.

No eyes meeting across a ballroom.

No rain.

No destiny.

She had 8% battery and he owned USB-C.

"You can use mine."

"Are you sure?"

"It's a charger, Nora. Not a kidney."

"I don't know you well enough to know how emotionally attached you are to your electronics."

"Deeply. Please respect us."

She laughed.

He smiled.

That should have been the end of it.

Instead, he messaged her the next morning.

**your phone survive?**

Nora replied:

**barely. she's brave.**

Adam:

**tell her i said get well soon**

She smiled at her screen.

This is how it begins.

Not with violins.

With nonsense.

## Then they started talking every day

At first it was casual.

A meme.

A song.

A complaint about Monday.

Then one morning Nora woke to:

**coffee before work?**

After that, coffee happened twice a week.

Then dinner.

Then Saturday markets.

Then an evening where they watched one movie and somehow talked for four hours afterward.

Adam started calling when he drove home.

Nora started sending voice notes instead of texts.

His toothbrush did not live at her apartment.

But his preferred sparkling water somehow did.

This was apparently the line they had chosen.

"It's not serious," Nora told her friend Gia.

Gia looked at her.

"You meal-prepped for him."

"I made too much pasta."

"You used two different containers and labelled his."

"Organisation is not love."

"You put a heart beside his name."

"It was decorative."

Gia stared.

Nora folded her arms.

"Stop."

"You're in a relationship."

"We're talking."

Gia took a sip of wine.

"To whom? The United Nations?"

## Adam was annoyingly consistent

![A warm romantic detail between two people](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1518199266791-5375a83190b7?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80 "Small routines becoming part of a relationship")

If he had been terrible, life would have been easier.

He was not.

He remembered when Nora had meetings.

Asked how her sister's exam went.

Showed up with iced coffee when she had a headache.

Waited until she got inside her apartment before driving away.

Never disappeared for three days and returned with:

**wyd**

like a Victorian ghost haunting an iPhone.

He was present.

Warm.

Reliable.

Which somehow made the lack of definition more confusing.

Nora understood red flags.

Red flags were efficient.

You saw them.

You complained to your friends.

You left.

Adam was mostly green flags attached to a man refusing to label the garden.

## The first time she got jealous was deeply embarrassing

They were at a rooftop bar with friends.

A woman named Sophie joined the group.

Beautiful.

Confident.

The sort of person who looked like she had never accidentally opened the front camera from below.

Sophie knew Adam.

Apparently very well.

She hugged him.

Nora noticed.

Which was fine.

Adults hug.

Civilisation continued.

Then Sophie touched his arm while laughing.

Still fine.

Nora's brain:

**Enemy aircraft detected.**

She immediately hated herself.

Adam sat beside Nora ten minutes later.

"You okay?"

"Perfect."

He looked at her.

"That voice means you're planning my death."

"Who is Sophie?"

There it was.

Elegant.

Subtle.

A diplomat.

Adam blinked.

"Friend from university."

"Mhm."

"Nora."

"What?"

"Are you jealous?"

She nearly inhaled her own tongue.

"No."

His mouth twitched.

"You are."

"I'm literally not."

"You've said 'literally' twice. You're cooked."

"Shut up."

He laughed.

Then, quietly:

"You know I'm not interested in Sophie, right?"

Nora looked at him.

The noise of the rooftop suddenly seemed farther away.

"Why would I need to know?"

Adam held her gaze.

There are questions people ask when they want information.

Then there are questions people ask because admitting the truth directly feels too dangerous.

Adam recognised the second kind.

He leaned closer.

"Maybe because I want you to."

Nora's heartbeat immediately became unserious.

Then somebody called his name.

The moment disappeared.

Of course it did.

Their entire relationship seemed professionally committed to almost happening.

## The internet was no help

Nora searched:

**how long should talking stage last**

Then:

**signs talking stage becoming relationship**

Then:

**can a man like you and still not ask you out**

Then, at 2:03 a.m.:

**how to stop being delulu respectfully**

Google had answers.

Unfortunately, none of them were Adam.

That was the irritating thing about relationship advice.

You could read fifty lists.

Watch twelve TikToks.

Ask three friends.

Study attachment theory like you were preparing for an exam.

Eventually, the person you wanted information about remained the only useful source.

Very inconvenient.

## Then Adam went quiet

Not ghosting.

Nothing dramatic.

He just became busier.

A major project at work.

Later replies.

One cancelled dinner.

Then another.

Nora told herself she understood.

She did understand.

She also did not enjoy it.

Both things were allowed to exist.

One evening she sent him a photograph of a café they had wanted to try.

**this weekend?**

Three hours passed.

Then:

**might be working :( can i let you know?**

Nora looked at the message.

It was perfectly reasonable.

Her feelings were less reasonable.

Because ambiguity is easy when everything feels good.

The moment something changes, every unanswered question wakes up.

What right did she have to feel disappointed?

They were not together.

What right did she have to expect his time?

They were "talking."

What exactly did that mean after ninety-eight days?

Apparently enough to miss someone.

Not enough to know whether you were allowed to say so.

## She stopped pretending she was chill

Friday night, Adam called.

"Hey."

"Hey."

"You sound weird."

Nora almost said:

**I'm fine.**

The ancient phrase.

Destroyer of conversations.

Instead she said:

"I don't think I like this anymore."

Silence.

Adam's voice changed.

"Like what?"

"This."

She sat on the edge of her bed.

"The talking stage. Whatever we're doing."

Another pause.

Nora's stomach dropped.

Maybe this was it.

Maybe she had misunderstood everything.

Maybe Adam was about to give her one of those speeches where a man says:

**I really like you, but I'm not ready for anything serious**

after behaving like somebody's husband for twelve weeks.

"Nora."

"What?"

"Can I come over?"

She blinked.

"Now?"

"Yes."

"It's almost midnight."

"I know."

"Adam."

"Please."

She closed her eyes.

"Fine."

## He arrived seventeen minutes later

Nora knew because she spent all seventeen minutes walking around her apartment doing useless tasks.

Folded a blanket.

Unfolded it.

Washed one glass.

Checked herself in the mirror.

Immediately became annoyed that she had checked herself in the mirror.

At 12:07, the buzzer sounded.

Adam stood outside in a grey hoodie, hair slightly messy, looking like he had driven there while arguing with himself.

Nora opened the door.

Neither moved.

"Hi," he said.

"Hi."

"You want to end this?"

Nora crossed her arms.

"I said I don't like this."

"That's worse."

"No, it's not."

"It sounded worse in the car."

She almost smiled.

Refused.

Adam stepped inside.

"What don't you like?"

Nora laughed once.

Not happily.

"The fact I don't know what I'm supposed to be."

His expression softened.

"Nora—"

"No, let me finish."

She hated how nervous she sounded.

But she continued.

"You text me every morning. We see each other constantly. You get jealous. I get jealous. Your mother apparently knows about me."

"My mother knows about everyone."

"She asked what dessert I like."

"She's thorough."

"Adam."

"Sorry."

Nora took a breath.

"I like you. Like, actually like you. And I'm tired of acting like saying that gives you some kind of tactical advantage."

He stared at her.

"So if you don't want a relationship, that's okay. Seriously. But I need to know because I can't keep pretending this is casual when it doesn't feel casual to me."

The room went quiet.

Adam looked down.

Nora's heart sank.

Then he laughed.

Very softly.

Her eyes narrowed.

"Are you actually laughing?"

"No."

"You are."

"At myself."

"Excellent time for personal growth."

He looked back at her.

"I thought you didn't want a relationship."

Nora stared.

"What?"

"You kept calling it casual."

"Because you kept calling it casual."

"I was copying you."

"I was copying you!"

They both stopped.

Human evolution had worked very hard for this moment.

Two adults.

Two functioning nervous systems.

Nearly one hundred days.

And both had apparently based their entire romantic strategy on copying the other person's fear.

Nora put both hands over her face.

"Oh my God."

Adam started laughing properly.

"This is actually embarrassing."

"Leave."

"Nora."

"Immediately."

He stepped closer.

She lowered her hands.

He was smiling.

Not teasing.

Something softer.

"I've wanted to ask you for weeks."

Her stomach flipped.

"Ask me what?"

He moved closer.

Close enough that her irritation had competition.

"To be my girlfriend."

Nora blinked.

"You're joking."

"I'm definitely not."

"You waited until twelve fifteen in the morning?"

"You were going to break up with me."

"We weren't together."

"Exactly. Extremely stressful."

She laughed.

She could not help it.

## Then he got serious

Adam reached for her hand.

Not pulling.

Just holding it.

"Nora, I like you."

She looked at him.

"I know I'm bad at saying things before I'm sure."

"That's one way of describing it."

"But I don't want casual."

His thumb brushed over her knuckles.

"I haven't wanted casual for a while."

The room suddenly felt warmer.

Nora looked down at their hands.

"So you've just been walking around silently knowing that?"

"Basically."

"Men should be studied."

"We are."

"No. More aggressively."

He laughed.

She looked up.

"Ask properly."

His smile disappeared.

Not completely.

Just enough that she knew he understood.

"Okay."

Adam straightened.

"Nora."

"Yes?"

"Will you be my girlfriend?"

She made him wait.

Three seconds.

Possibly four.

For justice.

Then:

"Yeah."

He smiled so quickly that she knew making him wait had been worth it.

"Yeah?"

"Don't make me repeat myself."

He stepped closer.

"Can I kiss you?"

Nora tilted her head.

"You've kissed me before."

"Feels different now."

That answer got her.

Completely.

She reached up and hooked one finger into the collar of his hoodie.

"Then yes."

## It did feel different

The kiss was not technically new.

They had kissed before.

After dates.

Outside cars.

Once in an elevator where another passenger entered halfway through and both of them spent six floors pretending nothing had happened.

But this one felt different.

There was no question hiding behind it.

Adam's hand settled at her waist.

Nora leaned into him.

His other hand rested lightly against her cheek.

No rush.

No performance.

Just the strange relief of finally being inside the same version of the story.

When they separated, Nora kept her forehead against his.

"So we're done talking?"

Adam smiled.

"I hope we continue talking."

"You know what I mean."

"Yes."

"Good."

"Although technically—"

"Don't."

He kissed her again.

A wise strategic decision.

## The talking stage wasn't actually the problem

The next morning Nora deleted her Notes-app list.

Almost.

She kept number twenty-six.

**At what point does "talking" become a tax category?**

It made her laugh.

Because the talking stage itself had not been terrible.

She had liked getting to know him.

Liked the uncertainty at first.

The first messages.

The first coffee.

The first time somebody's name appearing on her screen made her smile without permission.

The problem came when the relationship changed and the language did not.

They had grown closer.

Their expectations had changed.

Their feelings had changed.

But both of them had continued calling it casual because casual felt safer than asking a question with consequences.

That was the trap.

Sometimes undefined feels exciting.

Sometimes undefined simply means two people are terrified of defining something they already care about.

## Three months later

Gia met Nora for lunch.

"So," she said, "how is your former talking-stage associate?"

"My boyfriend?"

Gia smiled.

"I just wanted to hear you say it."

"You're unbearable."

"How does it feel?"

Nora considered the question.

Adam still annoyed her.

Still sent voice notes when a normal text would have been perfectly adequate.

Still stole fries after claiming he was not hungry.

Still occasionally became quiet when work overwhelmed him.

The relationship did not become perfect because somebody finally used the word boyfriend.

But something became easier.

They could talk about expectations without pretending expectations did not exist.

They could say:

**I miss you.**

**That upset me.**

**Are we okay?**

**I need you tonight.**

Simple sentences.

Terrifying sentences.

Useful sentences.

"It feels calmer," Nora finally said.

Gia nodded.

"That's disgustingly healthy."

"I know."

"I'm disappointed."

"Same."

## The real relationship started with a question

People talk about defining a relationship as though the label itself changes everything.

It does not.

A label cannot manufacture trust.

It cannot force commitment.

It cannot turn somebody inconsistent into somebody reliable.

But clarity does something valuable.

It allows two people to stop negotiating with imaginary versions of each other.

Nora had spent weeks asking the internet what Adam felt.

Adam had spent weeks trying to decode Nora.

Both had access to the correct person the entire time.

Humans are amazing.

We invented satellites.

Heart surgery.

Artificial intelligence.

And still occasionally require ninety-eight days to ask:

**Do you actually want to date me?**

The answer will not always be yes.

That is the scary part.

But uncertainty is an answer too, if you stay inside it long enough.

Nora had finally decided she would rather know.

It turned out Adam felt the same.

He had simply been equally afraid.

Which was romantic.

And stupid.

Often, those two things are roommates.
