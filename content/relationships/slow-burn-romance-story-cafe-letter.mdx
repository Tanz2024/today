Maya believed in three things with unreasonable confidence.

Good coffee.

Deleting messages before she could send something humiliating.

And the idea that if a man wanted to talk to her, he possessed both a phone and opposable thumbs.

This philosophy had served her well.

Mostly.

It had not, however, prepared her for the pale pink envelope sitting on her usual café table on a Friday evening.

Her name was written across the front.

**Maya.**

No surname.

No explanation.

Just handwriting she knew immediately.

She stopped walking.

"Oh, absolutely not."

The woman at the next table looked up.

Maya smiled politely.

"Sorry. Not you. My terrible decision-making history."

The woman nodded in a way that suggested she understood completely.

Women have been communicating entire novels through one sympathetic nod since approximately the invention of men.

Maya sat down.

She did not touch the envelope.

Not yet.

Her latte arrived.

She thanked the waiter.

She checked Instagram.

She checked WhatsApp.

She checked Instagram again, because apparently something revolutionary might have happened in the previous eleven seconds.

The envelope remained there.

Patient.

Annoying.

Pink.

And written by Elias.

The man she had spent ninety-two days convincing herself she absolutely did not miss.

Which would have been more convincing if she had not counted the days.

## This had technically been a situationship

![A woman smiling during a quiet outdoor moment](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1524250502761-1ac6f2e30d43?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80 "A quiet café moment before the letter")

Technically.

Maya hated the word.

A situationship sounded like two people had accidentally wandered into emotional customs without the correct paperwork.

But that was more or less what had happened.

She met Elias at her friend's birthday dinner three months earlier.

He was twenty-seven.

She was twenty-five.

Old enough to communicate clearly.

Young enough, apparently, to choose not to.

He had been sitting opposite her wearing a black shirt with the sleeves rolled to his forearms.

Maya noticed the forearms.

This information was unnecessary.

Her brain filed it anyway.

"You're staring," he said.

"I am literally looking in your direction."

"Very intensely."

"You have a face. It's in front of me."

"Strong defence."

She disliked him immediately.

Unfortunately, she also wanted him to keep talking.

Which is how many problems begin.

## He had too much confidence

Elias had the irritating calmness of somebody who knew exactly when a joke had landed.

He never rushed to fill silence.

Never checked whether she was laughing.

Never explained himself twice.

This gave him an unreasonable amount of aura.

Maya considered this suspicious.

Halfway through dinner, he asked what she did.

She told him.

Then he remembered.

Not just the job.

The details.

Two weeks later, he asked how a presentation she had mentioned once had gone.

This was dangerous.

A handsome man was one thing.

A handsome man with memory?

Society was not equipped.

They started texting.

Nothing serious.

Obviously.

Just every day.

Morning.

Lunch.

Midnight.

Random memes.

Photographs of ugly dogs.

Complaints about work.

One voice note that lasted seven minutes because Elias apparently believed podcasts should be personalised.

Maya told herself they were friends.

Her best friend Lina told her she was delusional.

"You're basically dating."

"We haven't even kissed."

"That doesn't make this better."

"We're just talking."

"You know his Starbucks order."

"That's public information."

"Maya."

"Fine."

Lina stared at her.

Maya stared back.

Both women knew she was cooked.

## Then came the rain

![A couple spending time together over coffee](https://images.pexels.com/photos/3184418/pexels-photo-3184418.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600 "The kind of ordinary moment that becomes important later")

It happened on a Wednesday.

They had gone for dinner.

Then dessert.

Then somehow walked forty minutes even though both of them had cars.

The universe, having no respect for subtlety, started raining exactly when they reached Maya's building.

They stopped beneath the awning.

Elias stood closer than usual.

Not touching.

Which was honestly worse.

There should be laws about standing that close to someone while smelling good.

Maya looked at him.

He looked at her.

Then his eyes dropped to her mouth.

Once.

Briefly.

But she saw it.

Every woman knows that look.

Your brain can be discussing taxes and suddenly every cell in your body goes:

**Oh.**

"Elias."

"Mhm?"

"What are you doing?"

"Thinking."

"That looks dangerous."

"For me, probably."

He stepped half an inch closer.

Maya could feel the warmth of him despite the rain cooling everything around them.

"About what?"

His voice lowered.

"Whether you're going to run away if I kiss you."

There are moments when a sophisticated adult woman should say something devastatingly clever.

Maya said:

"Oh."

Elite performance.

No notes.

He smiled.

She hated that smile.

Mostly because she wanted to kiss it off his face.

"Maybe," she whispered.

"Maybe you'll run?"

"Maybe you'll find out."

And then—

her phone rang.

Her sister.

Of course.

Civilisations have collapsed with better timing.

## Then he left

Three days later, Elias flew to Singapore for work.

Originally, it was supposed to be ten days.

Then fourteen.

Then nearly a month.

At first, nothing changed.

They texted.

Called.

He sent her pictures from hotel windows.

She sent him increasingly unflattering selfies while eating dinner.

Then work got heavier.

Calls got missed.

Replies got shorter.

One night Maya sent:

**you alive?**

Four hours later:

**barely 😭**

That was it.

She stared at the message.

Then at his Instagram story uploaded seventeen minutes earlier.

Ah.

The ancient ritual.

Too busy to reply.

Not too busy to post.

Wonderful.

Her frontal lobe packed its belongings and left.

She did not argue.

She did something worse.

She became polite.

**hope work gets better x**

He reacted with a heart.

A heart.

The emoji equivalent of putting flowers on a grave.

## Pride is a very expensive hobby

Elias called two nights later.

Maya let it ring.

He messaged:

**everything okay?**

She replied:

**yeah just busy**

A classic.

Nothing says emotional maturity like punishing someone for poor communication by communicating even worse.

They continued like this for another week.

Two people who obviously liked each other conducting diplomacy through lowercase messages.

Eventually, the conversation died.

No fight.

No breakup.

No dramatic speech.

Just silence.

That was almost worse.

You can hate somebody after an argument.

Silence leaves too much room for imagination.

Maybe he lost interest.

Maybe she had overreacted.

Maybe there was somebody else.

Maybe the whole thing had been significantly more serious in her head.

So Maya did what sensible people do.

She moved on.

Or, more accurately, she stopped talking about him and hoped everybody would confuse that with moving on.

## Ninety-two days later

The envelope sat beside her coffee.

Maya finally picked it up.

Her pulse immediately became embarrassing.

"This is so cringe," she whispered.

She opened it anyway.

The first sentence read:

> I tried writing this as a text six times.

She smiled.

Against her will.

> Every version sounded like I was either resigning from a company or asking you to renew a phone contract.

Okay.

Still him.

She continued.

> I owe you an apology before I owe you anything romantic.
>
> I disappeared into work and expected you to somehow understand what I wasn't explaining.

Her smile faded.

> Then when you pulled away, I convinced myself you didn't care.
>
> Which was convenient because it meant I didn't have to admit that I did.

Maya stopped reading.

Her coffee suddenly required inspection.

Her throat felt tight.

Annoying.

Absolutely unnecessary.

She returned to the letter.

> I liked you from the first night.
>
> I liked you when you spent twenty minutes pretending you weren't staring at me.
>
> I liked you in the rain.
>
> I especially liked you five seconds before your sister ruined my life.

Maya laughed out loud.

Several people looked over.

She did not care.

> I should have kissed you anyway.

Her stomach flipped.

Actually flipped.

Which was ridiculous.

She was twenty-five years old.

Her internal organs needed to behave professionally.

## "You tuck your hair when you're nervous"

She kept reading.

> You always tuck your hair behind your left ear when you're nervous.
>
> You order coffee too hot and then complain that it's hot.
>
> You pretend you hate romantic things and then cry at proposal videos.
>
> And you say "I'm fine" in a completely different voice when you're definitely not fine.

Maya immediately removed her hand from her hair.

The man was not even present and still somehow irritating her.

> I don't know if I've waited too long.
>
> Maybe you don't feel this anymore.
>
> Maybe you're seeing someone.
>
> If so, I'll leave you alone.
>
> But if there's even a small part of you that still wonders what would have happened if I kissed you that night—
>
> look behind you.

Maya froze.

No.

Absolutely not.

She turned.

Elias was sitting three tables away.

Watching her.

He lifted one hand.

Maya stared.

Then mouthed:

**You're insane.**

He nodded.

Fair.

## He looked nervous

That was new.

Elias crossed the café toward her.

Usually, he moved like somebody who had already read the script.

Not tonight.

Tonight his confidence appeared to have left the group chat.

He stopped beside the table.

"Hi."

Maya folded the letter.

"Hi?"

"I had a better opening prepared."

"You've had three months."

"Exactly. Pressure."

She wanted to stay angry.

Truly.

She had rehearsed this moment.

In several versions, she was icy.

Elegant.

Emotionally impenetrable.

In one version she had walked past him wearing sunglasses for reasons unclear even to herself.

Instead, she looked at him and said:

"You could've called."

His expression changed.

"I know."

"Or texted."

"I know."

"Or communicated like an adult human man."

"That's where you've become unrealistic."

She almost smiled.

Almost.

"Elias."

"I'm sorry."

And there it was.

No joke.

No charm.

No defence.

Just the thing she had wanted three months ago.

An honest sentence.

## Attraction is extremely inconvenient

The problem with unresolved attraction is that time does not always kill it.

Sometimes time marinates it.

This is deeply unfair.

Maya looked at him across the small marble table.

Same dark eyes.

Same hands.

Same mouth she had definitely not thought about kissing approximately eight hundred times.

He leaned forward.

Not much.

Enough.

Her body remembered the rain before her brain had finished processing the café.

"You look different," he said.

"Better?"

"More dangerous."

"Correct answer."

His eyes moved over her face.

Then stopped on her mouth.

Again.

There it was.

That look.

Three months disappeared instantly.

Maya hated chemistry.

You could have excellent boundaries, emotional intelligence, a skincare routine and a five-year plan.

Then somebody looked at your lips for half a second and suddenly civilisation was optional.

## "Why now?"

She needed to ask.

"Why now?"

Elias looked down at his hands.

"I got back two weeks ago."

Her eyebrows rose.

"Two weeks?"

"I came here four times."

Maya blinked.

"What?"

"I came here. You weren't here."

She stared.

"That's..."

"Pathetic?"

"I was going to say slightly unhinged."

"That's fair."

"And tonight?"

"I figured if you weren't here again, I'd finally text."

"So your grand romantic plan was stalking my coffee schedule?"

"Stalking feels legally aggressive."

"Monitoring?"

"Worse."

She smiled.

He saw it.

The tension shifted.

Not gone.

Just warmer.

## The café closed around them

They talked for nearly two hours.

About Singapore.

About the silence.

About the messages neither of them sent.

Maya admitted she had been hurt.

Elias admitted he had assumed distance meant rejection.

Neither explanation made everything magically fine.

But reality was strangely less dramatic than the stories they had both invented.

At eleven, the waiter politely informed them the café was closing.

They stepped outside.

The city had gone soft around the edges.

Streetlights reflected against wet pavement from an earlier shower.

The air smelled faintly like rain.

Of course it did.

The universe had jokes.

They walked slowly.

Neither mentioned where.

Then Elias stopped.

Maya took two more steps before turning.

"What?"

He stood under the amber streetlight.

Hands in his coat pockets.

Suddenly nervous again.

"I don't want to do the almost thing anymore."

Her heart misbehaved.

"Elias—"

"I mean it."

He came closer.

Slowly.

No assumptions.

No grabbing.

No performance.

He stopped directly in front of her.

"Can I kiss you?"

That question did something to her.

Not because it was dramatic.

Because after months of guessing, somebody had finally just asked.

Maya looked up at him.

"You wrote an entire letter before asking me that?"

"I panic creatively."

She laughed.

Then slipped her fingers into the front of his jacket.

"Yes."

## This time there was no phone call

His hand settled carefully against her waist.

Warm.

Steady.

He kissed her softly first.

Almost cautious.

Which lasted approximately two seconds.

Maya pulled him closer.

His other hand touched her jaw.

The kiss deepened, still slow, still deliberate, but carrying three months of unfinished conversation with it.

No fireworks.

No orchestra.

No conveniently timed cinematic thunder.

Just the quiet sound of traffic somewhere behind them and Maya's brain temporarily losing access to language.

When they separated, Elias rested his forehead lightly against hers.

Neither spoke.

Then Maya whispered:

"Okay."

He laughed.

"That's all I get?"

"What do you want? A Yelp review?"

"Five stars would be nice."

"Four."

"Brutal."

"Maybe four and a half."

"Growth."

She kissed him again before he could become more annoying.

## Romance is mostly terrible timing

Movies lie about romance.

They make it seem like love happens because two people meet at exactly the right moment.

Real life is less organised.

Sometimes you meet somebody while one of you is leaving.

Sometimes both of you are proud.

Sometimes somebody gets scared.

Sometimes a perfectly good almost-kiss gets destroyed because your sister wants to know whether you borrowed her charger.

The romantic part is not always destiny.

Sometimes it is simply two people deciding to stop being ridiculous at the same time.

That was Maya and Elias.

Not soulmates written in the stars.

Not fate.

Just two adults who liked each other enormously and had somehow managed to turn this simple fact into an administrative crisis.

## She kept the letter

Six months later, the envelope was still in Maya's bedroom drawer.

The corners were worn.

The fold down the middle had softened.

Elias knew she kept it.

What he did not know was that she occasionally reread it.

There are limits to the amount of power one should give a man.

They were not perfect.

He still disappeared into work sometimes.

Maya still overanalysed texts occasionally.

They argued.

Apologised.

Learned.

Once, during an argument, Elias said:

"You know, the letter was objectively romantic."

Maya replied:

"The letter was objectively desperate."

"You framed it."

She had, unfortunately.

That was beside the point.

## The embarrassing truth

Maya had spent years claiming romance was cringe.

Flowers?

Overrated.

Love letters?

Embarrassing.

Grand gestures?

Please stand up.

Then one pale pink envelope ruined her entire argument.

Because maybe romance itself was never embarrassing.

Maybe pretending you did not care was.

There are people who will spend months trying to look emotionally unavailable while checking whether one particular person watched their story.

People will type:

**lol it's fine**

while experiencing events that could reasonably qualify as an internal natural disaster.

Human beings are ridiculous like that.

Always have been.

The technology changes.

The foolishness remains undefeated.

And maybe that is the romantic part.

Not that somebody perfectly understands you.

Not that they never mess up.

Not that love arrives with flawless timing.

Maybe it is simply finding somebody who eventually stops running from the conversation.

Somebody who comes back.

Somebody who sits three tables away looking terrified while you read the truth they should have told you months ago.

Somebody who finally asks:

**Can I kiss you?**

And this time—

you don't let the phone ring.
