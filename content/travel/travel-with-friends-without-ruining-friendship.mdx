The best group trips are not built on everyone being identical. Talk about money, sleep, pace and alone time before you leave, so small differences do not become the whole trip.

## Agree on the non-negotiables

Set a rough daily budget, decide how you will split shared costs and name anything that matters—early starts, nightlife, food or rest.

## Leave space apart

A few hours doing separate things can protect the friendship. Togetherness is better when it is chosen, not enforced.

## Talk about money before the trip

Decide whether you are splitting everything evenly or paying for your own choices. Use a shared note or bill-splitting app for hotels, taxis and groceries. This avoids the classic “wait, who paid for that?” plot twist.

## Match your travel styles

One friend may want sunrise plans while another needs a slow morning. Agree on a few must-dos, then let everyone choose the rest. A good itinerary has anchors, not a military schedule.

## Have a repair plan

If the vibe gets tense, say it early and kindly. Try: “I think we are both tired—can we reset after lunch?” Clear words usually work better than silent resentment or a dramatic group-chat paragraph.

The goal is not a perfect trip. It is coming home with good memories and a friendship that still feels easy.

## Choose a decision system

Rotate who chooses lunch, transport and the next activity, or let the person who cares most about a plan lead that part. Shared decisions feel fairer when one friend is not secretly carrying the entire itinerary.
