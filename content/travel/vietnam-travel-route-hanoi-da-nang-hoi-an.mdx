Vietnam is having a major travel moment, and honestly, the hype makes sense. The country gives you chaotic city energy, unreal food, slow beach days and enough beautiful side streets to make your camera roll look professionally managed.

If it is your first trip, do not try to collect every city like a checklist. This route through Hanoi, Da Nang and Hoi An gives you variety without spending the entire holiday in transit.

## The route at a glance

**Hanoi → Da Nang → Hoi An** is a good nine-day shape: three nights for food and culture, three for beach and day trips, then three for lantern-lit evenings and a slower finish. Build in a little flexibility around weather and domestic travel days.

## Days 1–3: Start in Hanoi

Begin in Hanoi’s Old Quarter, where the best plan is usually to walk until something interesting happens. Get coffee early, explore Hoan Kiem Lake and let your first bowl of pho set the tone.

The city is busy, loud and full of tiny details. Look for family-run restaurants, late-night dessert spots and the streets that suddenly become completely different after sunset. Keep one afternoon free. Hanoi is better when you leave room for a random recommendation.

### What to do in Hanoi

- Walk around the Old Quarter before the streets get crowded.
- Try egg coffee if you want the trip’s most photogenic caffeine moment.
- Visit the Temple of Literature for a slower cultural stop.
- Take a food tour if choosing dinner feels impossible.

## Days 4–6: Slow down in Da Nang

Fly or take the train south to Da Nang, then switch from city wandering to beach mode. My Khe Beach is easy to reach, while the Marble Mountains add a little adventure without demanding a full expedition.

Da Nang works well as a reset. Spend the morning outside, eat seafood or noodles for lunch and keep the evening easy. You do not need to turn every day into a sunrise-to-midnight itinerary.

The city is also a useful base for nearby day trips. Check current transport and weather before booking, especially during rainy periods, because central Vietnam can change moods quickly.

### Getting from Hanoi to Da Nang

For most first-time travellers, the flight is the simplest way to protect the shape of the itinerary. Compare departure times and the baggage allowance before choosing the cheapest fare; an awkward connection or a bag fee can erase the saving. If you prefer scenery and have more time, trains are possible, but treat the journey as a travel day rather than a quick transfer.

## Days 7–9: Finish in Hoi An

Hoi An is the cinematic finale: yellow buildings, riverside lanterns and streets that feel made for an evening stroll. Go early if you want calmer photos, then return after dark when the lanterns change the whole atmosphere.

Use Hoi An for a slower final stretch. Browse local shops, take a cooking class, cycle through the countryside or simply find a café and watch the town move around you.

### One easy attraction day

Keep one half-day for the attraction you saved rather than filling every hour with transfers. Go early when possible, carry water and check the current ticketing information. A single well-chosen stop is usually more enjoyable than racing between five photo opportunities.

## How to make the route feel effortless

Book your first accommodation, major transport and one or two must-do activities. Leave the rest flexible. The best Vietnam memories may come from the place you found because your original plan had a gap in it.

Pack light, carry comfortable shoes and keep a rain layer close. A small bag makes domestic travel, taxis and spontaneous extra nights much easier.

![A traveller's bag, camera and map ready for a Vietnam trip.](https://images.pexels.com/photos/346885/pexels-photo-346885.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1400)

Also, check visa rules, local transport schedules and weather conditions before departure. Travel advice changes, so verify official information for your passport and route.

## The final itinerary

Start with Hanoi for culture and food, use Da Nang for breathing room, then end in Hoi An for the atmosphere. It is a balanced Vietnam route: enough movement to feel exciting, enough downtime to actually enjoy it.

Save the itinerary, send it to your group chat and then leave one day completely unplanned. That is usually where the good story starts.
