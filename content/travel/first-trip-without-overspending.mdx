Start with the total you can comfortably spend, then work backwards through transport, accommodation, food, local travel and an emergency buffer. A first trip feels much better when the budget is decided before the excitement takes over.

## Set the ceiling first

Choose a number that does not require debt or skipping essentials. Keep a separate buffer for delays, replacement documents and unexpected transport.

## Price the boring parts

Check baggage, airport transfers, taxes, meals and local transport—not just the headline ticket price. These are the costs that quietly move a trip over budget. Before you leave, use this [simple 3-day packing checklist](/travel/what-to-pack-for-a-3-day-trip) so you are not paying for an oversized bag full of things you never wear.

## Leave room for one good thing

A useful budget is not punishment. Reserve money for one meal, activity or experience you will genuinely remember.

## Compare the full trip cost

If you want to add up the real numbers before booking, use the [Trip Budget Planner](/tools/trip-budget-calculator) with your own transport, accommodation, food and buffer estimates.

When comparing destinations, add transport from the airport or station, accommodation fees, food, local travel and travel insurance. A cheap flight can stop being cheap after baggage and transfers. Search for the total, not just the headline price.

## Build a daily spending limit

Divide your flexible money across the number of days, then keep a small emergency buffer separate. You do not need to track every coffee forever—just notice where the budget is leaking before it becomes a jump scare.

## Book in the right order

Lock in the dates, transport and first accommodation before booking extras. Keep cancellation rules visible, especially if your plans are still a little “we’ll see.” A flexible plan is often cheaper than changing a rigid one later.

## Track the budget without killing the fun

Use a simple note with three lines: committed costs, daily spending and emergency money. Check it once a day, not after every snack. The point is knowing what you can still say yes to, not turning the trip into an accounting internship.
