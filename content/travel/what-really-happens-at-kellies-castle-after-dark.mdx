In daylight, Kellie’s Castle does not immediately look like somewhere you should be afraid of.

The red-brick walls rise above the greenery outside Batu Gajah, Perak. Sunlight pours through unfinished windows. Visitors move through open rooms, staircases and corridors taking photographs.

Then you imagine the same building after everyone has gone.

No crowds.

No voices.

Just empty hallways inside a mansion that was never finished.

And suddenly the stories people have been telling about Kellie’s Castle for decades become considerably harder to laugh at.

## The house that was never finished

Kellie’s Castle began as the dream of Scottish planter William Kellie Smith.

Smith had made his fortune in Malaya and planned an unusually ambitious home for his family. The mansion was supposed to have a six-storey tower, imported materials, rooftop entertaining space and even an elevator — an extraordinary feature in Malaya at the time.

But the house never became what he imagined.

Smith died from pneumonia in Lisbon in 1926 while travelling in Europe.

Construction stopped.

His family eventually left Malaya, and the unfinished mansion remained behind.

That part is history.

Everything that came afterwards is where things become less certain.

## The corridor people keep talking about

One of the longest-running stories around Kellie’s Castle involves a figure supposedly seen moving through the building.

Some visitors have described seeing what looks like a man in parts of the upper floors or along the corridors.

Over time, the figure became associated with William Kellie Smith himself.

It is an easy story to understand.

A man spends years trying to build an extraordinary home, dies before he can finish it, and is then supposedly seen walking through the place he never got to complete.

It almost sounds too perfectly written.

There is no reliable evidence proving that Smith’s ghost walks the castle.

But the story has survived for years.

And when you look down one of the long, empty corridors, you understand why.

## Watch the castle after dark

The footage below is not an AI recreation or a horror-film set.

It was filmed at the actual Kellie’s Castle during an after-dark visit.

The Malaysian Insight toured the building with its manager and explored some of the supernatural stories visitors and staff have attached to the property.

What you see is real.

What the unexplained stories mean is another question.

The darkness does a lot of work.

Rooms that look ordinary during visiting hours suddenly become black openings.

Long corridors disappear into shadow.

Every footstep carries farther than you expect.

And when someone tells you that people have reported seeing something standing at the other end of one of those corridors, your brain becomes extremely interested in every shape in the darkness.

## Then there is the story of Mr Gopal

This is one of the stranger stories connected to the castle.

In 2024, Bernama interviewed Kellie’s Castle managing director Zamari Muhyi about the site's growing association with dark tourism.

Zamari described an incident from a paranormal programme held at the castle in 2015.

According to his account, one participant suddenly began behaving unusually and introduced himself as “Mr Gopal.”

The story that emerged was of a South Indian laundry worker who supposedly wanted to return home to his wife.

The laundry area subsequently became one of the locations associated with the alleged presence.

There is an important detail here.

The story cannot be independently verified.

Even Bernama's report makes that uncertainty clear.

That makes it a ghost story, not established history.

But it is exactly the kind of story that grows around a building like this.

A name.

A room.

A strange incident.

Then years of visitors entering that same room already knowing what supposedly happened there.

## Visitors have described being touched and hearing whispers

Other reported experiences are less dramatic but arguably creepier.

Participants in paranormal visits have claimed to experience things such as whispers, unexpected touches and strange sensations inside the mansion.

Those experiences are subjective.

A dark historic building can create very real physical reactions even without anything supernatural happening.

Your hearing becomes more sensitive.

Small sounds become important.

A breeze feels like movement.

Your eyes try to turn shadows into recognisable shapes.

That does not mean people are lying about what they felt.

It means feeling something and knowing what caused it are two different things.

## A photograph added another story

The castle's reputation has also been strengthened by strange photographs.

In 2020, Malaysian media reported on a photographer who noticed what appeared to be a long-haired figure in the background of wedding photographs taken at Kellie’s Castle.

The photographer said he only noticed it later while editing.

Could it have been another person?

A trick of perspective?

A shadow?

Something stranger?

There is no reliable way to establish that from the story alone.

But once an image like that enters the mythology of a supposedly haunted building, it tends to stay there.

## The building itself might be the scariest part

You do not actually need a ghost to make Kellie’s Castle unsettling.

It is unfinished.

That matters.

Completed buildings usually tell you what spaces are supposed to be.

A bedroom looks like a bedroom.

A lobby looks like a lobby.

An unfinished mansion is different.

Doorways lead into empty rooms.

Stairs seem to disappear into other parts of the structure.

Arches frame darkness instead of furniture.

There are spaces that feel as though somebody stopped working on them yesterday rather than a century ago.

Then add the knowledge that the person who planned all of it never saw it finished.

Your imagination has everything it needs.

## So is Kellie’s Castle actually haunted?

There is no responsible way to say yes.

There are stories.

There are reported experiences.

There are photographs people interpret differently.

There is footage from people exploring the property after dark.

And there is more than enough atmosphere.

But none of that proves the supernatural explanation.

Maybe that is actually what makes Kellie’s Castle interesting.

If somebody could definitively prove there was a ghost standing in one specific corridor every night at 2:13 a.m., the mystery would almost disappear.

Instead, the place sits somewhere between history and folklore.

You can walk through the same rooms.

Stand in the same corridors.

Look into the same shadows.

And decide for yourself how much of the feeling comes from the building —

and how much comes from knowing the stories before you entered.

## The part that stays with you

The creepiest thing about Kellie’s Castle may not be any single ghost story.

It is the idea of an enormous home frozen halfway through becoming real.

William Kellie Smith intended it to be full of life.

Family.

Guests.

Parties.

New technology.

Instead, more than a century later, strangers walk through its empty rooms discussing people who may or may not still be there.

Go during the day and it is one of Perak’s most unusual pieces of architecture.

Imagine those corridors after dark and the building becomes something else entirely.

Whether that something is paranormal depends on what you believe.

But if you hear footsteps behind you when the corridor is empty?

You can decide how calmly you want to investigate.
