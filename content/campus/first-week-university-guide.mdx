The first week of university throws a lot at you at once — new buildings, new people, new systems for everything from laundry to registering for classes — and almost none of it needs to be figured out perfectly on day one. Most of what feels urgent that week actually has more slack in it than it seems.

## Figure out your physical logistics first

Before anything social or academic, get comfortable with the basics: where your classes actually are (walk the route once before the first day if you can), how laundry works in your building, where to get food when the dining hall is closed, and how to get around campus without your phone if it dies. These small logistics quietly cause more stress than academics in week one if they're not sorted.

## Don't panic about syllabus week

Most classes move slowly the first week — intros, syllabus reviews, maybe a light reading. It's not a fair sample of how intense the semester will actually be, so don't judge your workload or your ability to handle it based on week one. Save your energy for weeks two and three, when the real pace usually starts.

<TryThis>
Write down deadlines from every syllabus in one place immediately. You will not remember them later, and "one place" beats five different class portals you'll forget to check.
</TryThis>

## You don't need to unpack your whole life socially at once

It's tempting to feel like you need to lock in a friend group by Friday. You don't. The people you click with in week one might not even be the people you're close to by November — everyone's still figuring out who they are away from home, and that includes the people you just met too. Stay open rather than rushing to commit to the first group you find.

## Keep a very small, boring routine

Amid all the newness, one predictable habit — a set bedtime, a regular time you check in with family, a specific spot you always study — gives your brain an anchor when everything else is unfamiliar. It sounds unglamorous, but it does more for how settled you feel than any single big first-week event.

## Ask "dumb" questions early

Where's the health center, how do office hours actually work, what happens if you miss a class — these questions feel embarrassing to ask in week three once you're "supposed to" know, but they're completely normal in week one, when literally everyone is new. Ask them while it's socially free to not know things.

## Homesickness is common, not a red flag

Feeling off, missing home, or wondering if you made the wrong choice in the first week doesn't mean university is a mistake — it usually means you're adjusting to a genuinely big change, and that takes longer than a week regardless of how excited you were beforehand. Give it real time before drawing conclusions about how things are going to go.

Not a full friend group, not a perfect study system, not total confidence navigating campus. Just: you know where your classes are, you've written down your deadlines, and you've talked to at least a few new people. Everything else has the rest of the semester to sort itself out.
