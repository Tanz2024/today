There are some shows where you need three paragraphs of explanation before deciding whether you care.

The Scandal is not really one of them.

A brilliant noblewoman.

One of Joseon's most notorious lovers.

A woman who was never supposed to become part of their game.

And one terrible idea involving a romantic wager.

That is basically enough information to understand why Netflix's new Korean period drama has people's attention.

The Scandal premiered globally on Netflix on September 18, starring Son Ye-jin, Ji Chang-wook and NANA. It takes a story about desire, manipulation and people making extremely questionable romantic decisions and drops it into Joseon-era high society.

So if it suddenly started appearing on your Netflix screen and your feed at the same time, here is what is actually going on.

## What is Netflix's The Scandal about?

The story revolves around Lady Cho, played by Son Ye-jin.

She is intelligent, wealthy and living inside a society that gives women considerably less freedom than she would like.

Then there is Cho Won, played by Ji Chang-wook.

His reputation is basically the opposite of subtle.

He is known as a skilled seducer, and Lady Cho pulls him into a dangerous bet involving Hui-yeon, played by NANA.

Hui-yeon becomes the target of their game.

What begins as manipulation obviously has the potential to become much messier once actual feelings enter the situation.

This is not really:

"Will they fall in love?"

It is more:

"How badly can this entire plan collapse once everybody stops following the plan?"

Much more interesting.

## The cast is a big part of the attraction

Netflix did not exactly hide the star power here.

Son Ye-jin leads as Lady Cho, while Ji Chang-wook plays Cho Won and NANA plays Hui-yeon. Netflix also lists Han Seon-hwa among the cast. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

Even before getting into the story, that combination gives the series an obvious audience.

Son Ye-jin is taking on a character with intelligence, status and hidden motives.

Ji Chang-wook gets the dangerous-charmer role.

And NANA enters the middle of a game she did not design.

You can already see where the emotional damage may be heading.

## This is not the comforting kind of historical romance

If your idea of a period drama is mostly beautiful clothes, lingering glances and people politely falling in love near a palace wall, adjust expectations.

The Scandal is built around manipulation.

Lady Cho proposes the wager.

Cho Won accepts it.

Hui-yeon becomes entangled in something she does not fully understand.

Netflix itself describes the show as a dangerous love game involving hidden desires and a risky bet. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

So yes, there are beautiful costumes and historical settings.

There are also people emotionally playing with fire and apparently deciding to stand closer to it.

## There is an older story underneath it

The drama is a new interpretation of Pierre Choderlos de Laclos's 1782 novel Dangerous Liaisons.

That story has survived for centuries for a fairly obvious reason.

Rich, clever people manipulating romance for entertainment is unfortunately very reusable material.

The Scandal moves that basic idea into Joseon-era Korea and gives it a modern dramatic perspective while keeping the central game of seduction, power and consequences. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

So even though the setting is historical, the central mess is extremely easy to understand.

Somebody thinks they can control feelings.

Feelings disagree.

## Why the Joseon setting actually matters

Putting this story inside Joseon society does more than make everything look beautiful.

Reputation, gender and social expectations carry much bigger consequences for the characters.

Lady Cho is presented as a woman whose abilities push against the restrictions placed on women of her era.

Cho Won can move through society with freedoms that are not equally available to the women around him.

And Hui-yeon's position makes becoming part of their wager more dangerous than simply having an awkward dating experience.

That imbalance gives the romantic game actual stakes.

This is not somebody getting left on read.

Lives and reputations can fall apart.

Dating apps suddenly seem peaceful.

## The show knows exactly what kind of mess it is selling

There is something very watchable about characters who are clearly intelligent making decisions you can already tell are going to create problems.

The setup basically tells you disaster is somewhere ahead.

The entertainment is watching how everybody gets there.

Lady Cho believes she can control the game.

Cho Won believes he can win it.

Hui-yeon is supposed to be part of the strategy.
