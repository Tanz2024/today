The wilted rose emoji (🥀) means something is sad, embarrassing, or over in a way that's meant to be a little funny rather than genuinely sympathetic. It's the opposite of the fresh rose emoji's romantic meaning — this one signals decline, defeat, or secondhand embarrassment, usually dropped as a reaction rather than typed out as words.

## How it's actually used

You'll see it under a video of someone getting rejected, losing badly at a game, or posting something that didn't get the reaction they hoped for. It functions like a sympathetic "oof" or "rip," but with a visual punch that a plain text reaction doesn't have — dropping just the emoji, with no caption, is often funnier than actually writing a sentence about it.

## Why a flower means "that's rough"

The wilting itself carries the meaning: a rose that's died or drooped is a fast, recognizable visual metaphor for something that used to have potential and doesn't anymore — a crush that didn't work out, a plan that fell apart, a moment that should have gone well and didn't. Emoji-based slang often works this way, borrowing a visual that already carries an obvious feeling and repurposing it as shorthand.

<GoodToKnow>
Context changes the tone a lot. Dropped under a stranger's post, it can read as mocking. Dropped under a close friend's post about their own bad day, it usually reads as a joking "same, I feel that" rather than an insult.
</GoodToKnow>

## Where it fits with similar reactions

It sits alongside other single-emoji reaction shorthand like 💀 (something's so funny or bad it's "killing" you) or 😭 (secondhand embarrassment or overwhelming emotion, not necessarily sadness). None of these are meant to be taken literally — they're fast, low-effort ways to react to something without writing anything out, which is most of the appeal of using them at all.

## Should you actually use it?

If it fits the moment and the person you're replying to would find it funny rather than mean, it's a quick, low-stakes way to react. Like most reaction slang, it works best used sparingly and dropped at the right moment — spamming any single emoji reaction under everything tends to flatten it from a joke into just noise.
