Comparing yourself online is basically your brain doing unpaid detective work on a stranger’s best twelve seconds. The feed is edited, filtered, and strategically posted. It is not the full lore.

## Notice the trigger

Do you spiral after seeing a perfect room, relationship, body, or career update? Name the trigger instead of letting it quietly run the show.

## Change the feed, not your entire personality

Mute accounts that make you feel permanently behind. Follow people who teach, make you laugh, or remind you that normal life is not an aesthetic failure.

## Use comparison as information

Ask what the post is showing you that you want: more creativity, better routines, new friends, or a change of scene. Take the useful clue and drop the self-drag.

## The bottom line

Your life is not losing because someone else posted first. Log off, do one thing that belongs to you, and let your timeline stop writing your self-worth.

## Why comparison hits so hard

Social feeds show outcomes without the awkward drafts, money, help or luck behind them. Your brain compares your behind-the-scenes with someone else’s highlight reel and calls the result evidence. It is not.

## Make your feed less loud

Mute accounts that trigger spiralling, unfollow content that makes you feel behind and add creators who teach, make you laugh or show realistic process. You do not need to announce the cleanup. Protecting your attention is enough.

## Measure your own progress

Keep a private list of small wins: replying to the email, learning a skill, resting properly or showing up again. Progress can be quiet and still count.
