Being exhausted but wide awake is a special kind of betrayal. Your body wants sleep; your brain has opened a 2 a.m. board meeting.

## Your schedule may be out of sync

Late nights, naps, travel, and inconsistent wake times can shift your body clock. One rough night usually needs consistency, not panic.

## Your brain may still be on alert

Stress, scrolling, caffeine, and tomorrow’s worries can keep your system activated. Try a dim, quiet wind-down before bed instead of another intense feed session.

## Stop forcing it

If you are awake and frustrated, get up briefly and do something calm in low light. Return when you feel sleepy again.

## When to get support

If sleep problems keep happening, affect your daytime life, or come with other symptoms, talk with a healthcare professional. You deserve help, not a nightly boss battle.

## The bottom line

Sleep is not a test you fail by being awake. Make the room boring, keep your schedule steady, and get support when the pattern will not let up.

## A better wind-down routine

Start with the same three steps most nights: dim the lights, put the phone away from the bed and do something quiet for a few minutes. Reading, stretching or listening to calm audio can give your brain a softer landing than doomscrolling until your eyes give up.

## Check the daytime basics

Morning light, regular movement and a consistent wake time can help your body recognise when night is actually night. Keep caffeine earlier if it affects you, and avoid turning one bad night into a week of panic.

## When sleep needs proper support

Talk to a healthcare professional if insomnia lasts for weeks, snoring or breathing problems wake you, or tiredness makes daily life unsafe. Sleep advice online is useful, but persistent problems deserve a real assessment.
