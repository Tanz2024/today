In Malaysia, Befrienders KL offers confidential emotional support at **03-7627 2929**; elsewhere, use [Find a Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/) to find a local service. If you may act soon, call emergency services now.

This article discusses depression and suicide in a non-graphic way. You can skip any section that feels too close to home.

Depression does not always look like crying in bed. For many people in Gen Z, it can look like going quiet in group chats, feeling permanently tired, becoming irritable, missing classes or shifts, losing interest in things that used to matter, or feeling numb while still posting and showing up.

The important part is not diagnosing a friend from the outside. It is noticing a change, taking it seriously and helping them connect with support.

## Depression is not a personality flaw

The World Health Organization says depression, anxiety and behavioural disorders are among the leading causes of illness and disability among adolescents. Depression can affect sleep, appetite, concentration, motivation and the ability to feel pleasure. It can also sit beside stress about money, study, work, family, identity, discrimination, relationships or the future.

Mental health struggles are not proof that someone is weak, dramatic or failing at adulthood. They are health concerns, and treatment and support can help.

## What suicidal thoughts can sound like

People do not always say, “I want to die.” They might say:

- “Everyone would be better off without me.”
- “I cannot do this anymore.”
- “I want everything to stop.”
- “There is no point in planning for the future.”

One sentence alone cannot tell you exactly what is happening, but a sudden change, hopelessness, giving away important things, saying goodbye, looking for ways to die, or withdrawing sharply deserves a calm and direct response.

## Ask directly, without panic

Try: “I have noticed you seem really overwhelmed lately. Are you thinking about suicide?”

The question may feel frightening to say out loud, but direct, non-judgmental language gives the person room to answer honestly. You do not need to debate them, promise to keep a dangerous secret, or fix everything in one conversation.

Listen. Thank them for telling you. Ask whether they are in immediate danger, whether they have a plan, and whether they have access to anything they could use to hurt themselves. If the answer suggests immediate danger, do not leave them alone if you can safely stay, reduce access to immediate means, and bring in an adult, clinician, crisis service or emergency service.

## What helps when someone says they are not okay

Keep the next step small and specific:

- Sit with them while they text a trusted person, counsellor or doctor.
- Offer to go with them to a clinic, campus counselling service or emergency department.
- Help them make a short plan for the next few hours: who will be nearby, where they will stay, and who they can call.
- Check in again tomorrow. Support is a pattern, not a one-time performance.

Avoid guilt trips such as “Think of your family,” forced positivity, or treating the person like a problem to manage. You can care deeply without becoming their only support. If you are supporting a friend, tell another trusted adult or professional when safety is at risk.

## Social media can help — and hurt

Online spaces can make it easier to find language, community and peer support. They can also amplify comparison, harassment, self-harm content and hopelessness. The WHO notes that digital media can either strengthen or weaken suicide-prevention efforts.

Curate aggressively when you are vulnerable: mute triggering accounts, leave harmful chats, report dangerous content, and follow people or services that encourage real-world support. A post is not a substitute for a clinician or crisis service.

## When to get urgent help

Treat it as urgent if someone says they intend to die soon, has a specific plan, has already hurt themselves, is intoxicated and unsafe, or cannot commit to staying safe. Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. Stay with them, or make sure another safe person is physically present.

If you are outside Malaysia, [Find a Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/) lists crisis services by country. In the United States and Canada, call or text **988**. If you are elsewhere and cannot find a local number, call your country’s emergency service.

## What to remember

You do not need the perfect words. Notice the change, ask clearly, listen without judgment, and connect the person to support. Depression is treatable, and suicidal feelings can change. Getting another human involved is a strong next step — for you or for someone you care about.
